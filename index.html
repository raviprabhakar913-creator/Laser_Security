Digital Electronics Project Specifications & Theory

Project: Laser Security Alarm System using LDR & NPN Transistor Switch

Authors: Shahid Ali & Ravi Shankar • Institution: Shri Ramswaroop Memorial University (SRMU)

Subject: Digital Electronics • Academic Mini-Project

1. Circuit Working Principle

A semiconductor laser diode (wavelength 650nm) projects an intense optical beam onto a Light Dependent Resistor (LDR). The LDR forms the bottom leg of a voltage divider with a preset potentiometer (RPRESET):

VB = VCC × [ RLDR / (RPRESET + RLDR) ]

When illuminated by the laser, RLDR ≈ 1 kΩ. Hence, VB < 0.7 V, holding the BC547 NPN transistor in Cutoff (collector current IC ≈ 0, Buzzer OFF). When an intruder obstructs the beam, RLDR spikes to > 100 kΩ, pulling VB ≥ 0.7 V. This drives the transistor into Saturation, completing the circuit to ground and energizing the 2.4kHz piezoelectric buzzer and LED alert.

2. Transistor Switch Characteristics

  • Cutoff State (VBE < 0.7V): IB = 0, IC = 0. Output logic is Y = 0. Load voltage across buzzer is 0V.
  • Saturation State (VBE ≥ 0.7V): Base-emitter forward-biased (VCE(sat) ≈ 0.2V). Output logic is Y = 1. Load voltage ≈ 4.8V, powering buzzer and alert LED.

3. Project Limitations & Academic Enhancements

  • Ambient Light Interference: In daylight or bright rooms, diffuse light keeps $R_{LDR}$ lower than in total darkness. If $R_{PRESET}$ is poorly adjusted, $V_{BE}$ may not reach $0.7\text{V}$ even when the laser is blocked (False Negative). Optical hoods and potentiometer tuning solve this.
  • Alarm Latch Need: Without a latch, the buzzer silences the instant the intruder passes the beam. Incorporating an SR Latch (555 IC or cross-coupled NAND gates) locks the alarm until reset by security staff.
  • Dual-Beam Coverage: A single beam can be ducked or stepped over. Dual parallel beams combined via digital OR logic ensure fail-safe perimeter enclosure.
